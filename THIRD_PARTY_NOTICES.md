# Third party data and code

Nothing listed here is redistributed by this repository. Every dataset is
downloaded on demand by a script in `scripts/`, and `data/` is ignored by git.
This section exists so a reader knows what they are about to download and under
what terms, and so the terms are recorded where they can be checked rather than
taken on trust.

## Every asset the page shows, and what it is

Some of what the page draws is observed, some is the model's output on observed points, and one thing
is synthetic. Each is labelled where it is shown.

| Asset | Provenance | Observed | Label shown on the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| The live scene, `data/viewer/` (built, not committed) | USGS 3DEP San Francisco points, coloured by a model's calls | the points, yes; the colours are predictions | the variant on the model label, for example "M0 seed A, the published model" |
| The photograph on the points | NAIP 2022 imagery, public domain, sampled at each point | yes, 337 days from the lidar | "the aerial photograph on the points, not the model" |
| The stills, `web/public/stills/` | Rendered by `scripts/pipeline/render_frames.py` from the same points and calls | the points, yes; the colours are predictions | the area and the variant, burned into each still |
| The water inset, `web/public/stills/water_inset_*.png` | swissSURFACE3D Zug and 3DEP Embarcadero points, coloured by M1 seed A's calls | the points, yes; the colours are predictions | "coloured by M1 seed A", and the swisstopo attribution |
| The FIXTURE octree, `web/fixtures/FIXTURE/` | Generated by `scripts/pipeline/build_fixture_octree.py` | no, synthetic points and random confidences | a FIXTURE banner on every screen |
| The design sheets, `docs/design/` | Screenshots of the running page, and the palette's evidence frames | the page as it rendered | named in `docs/design/README.md` |

## The training labels come with three licences, not one

This is the part that is easy to get wrong. The HuggingFace mirror this project
downloads is tagged `mit`. That tag does not describe the two layers underneath
it, and neither the tag nor the card mentions them.

| Layer | What it is | Licence | Where that is stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw points | The lidar itself, flown over Surrey | Open Government Licence, City of Surrey. Commercial use allowed, attribution required | DALES paper, section 7, Acknowledgement. Terms at [data.surrey.ca](https://data.surrey.ca/pages/open-government-licence-surrey) |
| DALES labels | The original 8-class hand annotation | "Creative Commons 3.0", with a separate sentence stating the data may not be used for commercial purposes | [DALES project page](https://sites.google.com/a/udayton.edu/vasari1/research/earth-vision/dales), under Copyright |
| mbendjilali/DALES-2 labels | The 15-class re-annotation this project trains on | MIT | [Dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mbendjilali/DALES-2), frontmatter and body |

Three things follow, and they are the reason this table exists.

**"Creative Commons 3.0" is not a licence identifier.** It names no variant. There
is no BY, no NC and no SA in it, and the non-commercial restriction arrives as a
separate hand-written sentence rather than as part of a named licence. So the
upstream label terms are prose, and they cannot be reduced to an SPDX string.

**The MIT tag on the mirror is not consistent with the layers under it.** The
mbendjilali/DALES-2 card describes its work as a human-in-the-loop revision of the DALES and
DALES Objects annotations. A revision of a non-commercially licensed annotation
does not straightforwardly become MIT. This is not a claim that the mirror is
wrong, it is a statement that the question is open and that the tag alone does
not settle it.

**There is no share-alike anywhere upstream.** No layer requires this repository
or anything derived from it to carry a particular licence. That is why the code
here is MIT and why that is not in tension with the above.

**What this project does about it.** The work is non-commercial in fact: a
portfolio repository, no product, no service, nothing sold. No tile is
redistributed. All three papers are cited. Anyone wanting to use this
commercially has to resolve layer 2 with the University of Dayton first, and the
DALES page says they consider that case by case.

The required attribution for the point data, reproduced exactly as the licence
specifies it:

> Contains information licensed under the Open Government License – City of Surrey.

That string contains an en dash. Repository prose in this project uses no dashes
of any kind, and this line is the single exception, because it is a legal
citation rather than prose. `tests/integrity/` exempts it by name so the
punctuation guard neither fails on a correct file nor quietly rewrites a legal
attribution.

**One more fact about this data, which belongs here as much as in the README.**
DALES stands for Dayton Annotated Laser Earth Scan. Dayton is the University of
Dayton, where the annotating was done. The lidar was flown over Surrey, British
Columbia, Canada, 3,198 km away. `scripts/investigations/where_is_dales.py` measures that from
the tiles themselves.

## The target city

**USGS 3DEP, delivery `CA_SanFrancisco_1_B23`**, read from
`s3://usgs-lidar-public`. United States Government Public Domain, anonymous
access, no attribution required. Downloaded by `scripts/pipeline/fetch_sf_aoi.py`.

The delivery is published as an Entwine Point Tile octree in EPSG:3857. It
carries ASPRS ground classification and nothing else: no building class and no
vegetation class, with 61 to 77 percent of points left unassigned depending on
the area.

Note that `s3://usgs-lidar`, without the `-public` suffix, is Requester Pays and
bills the caller. `backend/surrey/ept.py` refuses it, and
`tests/unit/test_ept.py` checks the refusal in both directions.

## The imagery

**NAIP 2022**, USDA Farm Production and Conservation Business Center, read from the public
NAIP collection. United States Government public domain. It colours the NAIP side of the
viewer's divider and builds the blind vegetation reference; it is never a training label.

## The Swiss training tiles (M0prime and M1)

**swissSURFACE3D**, Federal Office of Topography swisstopo, free geodata under swisstopo's
terms of use, which make a source reference mandatory wherever the data or anything made from
it is shown or passed on. The form used here: Federal Office of Topography swisstopo (©swisstopo).
Downloaded by `scripts/pipeline/prep_swiss.py`; no tile is redistributed.

## The water mask for DALES training tiles

**BC Freshwater Atlas**, Province of British Columbia, used only to leave DALES points over
water out of M0prime and M1 training (`scripts/pipeline/dales_water_check.py`). Open Government Licence,
British Columbia, whose required statement is reproduced exactly, dash included:

> Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – British Columbia.

## The sea level

**NOAA CO-OPS station 9414290, San Francisco, CA**, verified six minute water level
in NAVD88, from the CO-OPS data API. The NOAA disclaimer states that information on
its servers is in the public domain unless annotated otherwise, and NOS requests
attribution: water level data from NOAA National Ocean Service, Center for
Operational Oceanographic Products and Services. Downloaded by
`scripts/pipeline/water_null.py` and used only for the water null, never as a label. The water null is not a do-nothing baseline: it knows each point's absolute height in NAVD88 and the sea level at the moment of flight. The model knows neither; it sees height above the floor of a 40 m block.

## The building reference

**Microsoft Building Footprints**, ODbL-1.0. The California state file is
downloaded as a 466 MB zip and cached; `scripts/pipeline/extract_footprints.py` cuts out
the San Francisco subset.

## The weights and predictions this project makes

The trained weights and the predictions they make are licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 in `LICENSE_MODEL.md`,
apart from the MIT code licence. No layer above requires that, since none is share-alike; the
non-commercial term is passed on because the DALES label layer carries one, and whether weights
inherit a label licence is unsettled.

## What the outputs here are not

The colours this project puts on San Francisco points are **predictions from a
model trained on another country**. They are not measurements, not ground truth,
and not part of any USGS deliverable.

The ASPRS ground class used as a reference on San Francisco is the **delivery's
own automated classification**. It is not hand labelled and it is not a
benchmark annotation, so a score against it measures agreement with a vendor's
algorithm, not correctness.

Microsoft Building Footprints are a **2D reference of uncertain vintage**. A
footprint polygon contains whatever stands inside it, including ground, parked
cars and overhanging branches, so agreement with a footprint is not proof that a
point is part of a building. `scripts/pipeline/footprint_null.py` measures how much of
that agreement comes for free.

Nothing here is validated against surveyed ground truth in San Francisco,
because none is published for this delivery.
