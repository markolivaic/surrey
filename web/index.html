Golden Gate Park. The trees are trees.

The model learned from lidar flown over Surrey, British Columbia, and was never trained on San Francisco. Here it labels as vegetation nearly every point the aerial photograph shows as vegetation.

In the Sunset, where the photograph has plenty of both sides: of the vegetation, and of the rest.

In the park, over the seeds: to of the vegetation. The park has little the photograph shows as not vegetation, points, and the model labels of them vegetation; the method notes give why that figure is not read as a test.

A rule that calls every point 2 to 25 m up vegetation scores 100% on both sides, because the reference points are drawn from that height band. In the Sunset the model finds a little less of the vegetation than that rule, and takes in very little of the rest.

A point counts only inside a patch the photograph marks one way or the other, away from the patch's edge, because a tree in an aerial photograph leans away from the camera.
